# What other library-hours campaigns teach

Thirteen fights over library hours, what happened, and what carries over to DeKalb. Sources are linked; figures are as reported by local press or the governments involved.

## Campaigns that won

### DeKalb County, 2016 — the precedent at home
Hours cut in 2011 stayed cut for five years. In July 2016 the Board of Commissioners added $491,972 to the mid-year budget to restore night and weekend hours. From January 2017, 17 branches opened 111 more hours a week, at about $2 million a year.
**Carries over:** the mid-year budget is a working route in DeKalb, and commissioners have already voted to pay for hours. ([AJC](https://www.ajc.com/news/local-govt--politics/dekalb-libraries-add-night-and-weekend-hours-2017/9HkuC8Eu22nIf3EhPJGL9N))

### Fulton County, 2015
After a $5 million cut closed all but two branches on Fridays, residents complained loudly enough that the commission chair said the county got "an earful." The 2015 budget restored $1.9 million and 2013 hours, by unanimous vote, helped by revenue from a recent tax increase.
**Carries over:** steady constituent contact moves commissioners, and a yes is easier when new revenue has just arrived. DeKalb raised its tax rate in mid-2026. ([Rough Draft Atlanta](https://roughdraftatlanta.com/2015/02/09/pressure-county-pays-restore-library-hours/))

### Cobb County, 2018
A proposal to close or merge eight branches produced Save Cobb Libraries: a Facebook group started by one resident, then a website, a petition and packed meetings. Commissioners raised the tax rate, kept the branches and expanded hours, including Sunday hours at regional libraries.
**Carries over:** one person can start this. A named group with a simple site and a petition gave commissioners something to respond to. ([East Cobb News](https://eastcobbnews.com/save-cobb-libraries-citizens-group-launches-website/))

### New York City, 2023–2025
Mid-year cuts ended Sunday service in November 2023. The three library systems, council members and tens of thousands of residents campaigned through op-eds, rallies and testimony. The June 2024 budget restored $58.3 million and Sunday service returned within weeks; the 2025 budget added more seven-day branches.
**Carries over:** a broad coalition, a single number, and a legislature willing to make it a condition of the budget. ([NYC Council](https://council.nyc.gov/press/2024/07/14/2643/))

### Chicago, 2019
The mayor made Sunday hours at every branch a budget priority and paid for it with an $18 million property tax increase. All 77 branches were open Sunday afternoons by late 2021.
**Carries over:** every-branch Sunday service happens when the executive owns it and names the revenue. ([WTTW](https://news.wttw.com/2021/12/17/all-chicago-library-branches-open-sunday-afternoons-1st-time-2011))

### Washington, DC, 2013
Sunday hours had been cut everywhere but the main library in 2009. One council member proposed restoring them; the mayor adopted the idea in the budget and all branches reopened on Sundays that October.
**Carries over:** a single legislator who makes this their issue can be enough. ([DCist](https://dcist.com/story/13/07/30/dc-libraries-will-once-again-be-ope/))

### Carlsbad, California, 2025 and Santa Fe, New Mexico
Both restored Sunday hours through routine budget action, Carlsbad at a mid-year update.
**Carries over:** this does not have to wait for the annual budget. ([City of Carlsbad](https://www.carlsbadca.gov/Home/Components/News/News/3025))

### Alameda County, California — cities that pay for their own branch
Newark, Union City and Albany each pay the county library for extra hours at their branch. Newark's 2025 contract buys four Sunday hours.
**Carries over:** a model worth knowing about if DeKalb's cities want to add to what the county funds. It is not the campaign's ask, because it would open libraries only where a city can pay. ([Alameda County agenda](https://www.acgov.org/board/bos_calendar/documents/DocsAgendaReg_07_22_25/GENERAL%20ADMINISTRATION/Regular%20Calendar/Library%20(Alameda%20County)_389740.pdf))

## Campaigns that lost or stalled

### San Diego, 2025
Facing a structural deficit, the city closed libraries on Sundays and Mondays. Advocates won back Mondays at 17 branches. Sundays stayed closed; council members said the money could not be found.
**Warning:** when the government is cutting everywhere, even a well-run campaign recovers only part. DeKalb is not in that position, which is worth saying plainly. ([KPBS](https://www.kpbs.org/news/politics/2025/06/11/san-diego-city-council-restores-rec-center-and-some-library-hours-in-budget))

### Montgomery County, Maryland, 2026
The library ended Sunday hours at ten branches in January 2026. Its director said the reason was staffing and sustainable operations, not money. The library board and residents asked the council to restore $624,864; the approved budget kept the cut, and council members asked for usage data.
**Warning:** if library management says Sunday service is not workable, elected officials will defer to them. The campaign has to bring the library's leaders along and show how Sundays get staffed. ([WTOP](https://wtop.com/montgomery-county/2026/01/whats-behind-sunday-closures-at-all-but-9-montgomery-county-libraries-3/))

### Philadelphia, 2018–2025
Flat funding and layoffs left branches closing without notice for lack of staff. Even after money for weekend hours was approved, service returned branch by branch over two years because hiring took that long.
**Warning:** money alone does not open doors. A budget vote needs a hiring plan and an opening date attached. ([Billy Penn](https://billypenn.com/2023/02/23/free-library-philadelphia-saturday-hours-return/))

### Jackson County, Oregon and Dayton, Ohio, 2025–2026
Both cut Sunday hours when revenue fell.
**Warning:** Sunday is the first day cut when budgets tighten. Winning it is not the end; it has to be defended each year. ([Jefferson Public Radio](https://www.ijpr.org/politics-government/2026-06-12/jackson-county-libraries-cut-sunday-hours-eliminate-positions))

## Patterns

1. **An inside champion plus outside pressure.** Every win had an official who owned the idea and residents who kept showing up.
2. **One vote, one number.** Winners asked for a specific amount in a specific budget.
3. **Staffing decides it.** The strongest objection is operational. Where library leaders said it could be done, it happened.
4. **Revenue timing matters.** Wins cluster in years when new revenue was available.
5. **Restoring is easier than adding.** Most wins reversed a recent cut. DeKalb's ask is a new service, so it needs evidence of demand and a positive case.
6. **Broad coalitions win.** Parents, faith communities, schools, seniors and employers, not library regulars alone.
7. **Every-branch service needs an owner.** Chicago, DC and Gwinnett got there because someone in office made it theirs.
