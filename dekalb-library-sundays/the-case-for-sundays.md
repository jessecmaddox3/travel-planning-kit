# The case for Sundays

**Open every DeKalb County library on Sunday, noon to 5.**

*Open Sundays DeKalb — working paper, November 2026. Figures marked "we estimate" are the campaign's own and are set out in the [cost model](cost-model/). Sources for everything else are in [Facts and sources](research/01-facts-and-sources.md).*

## The short version

All 23 DeKalb County libraries are locked every Sunday. For most families that leaves one weekend window, Saturday from 10 to 5, to use buildings and collections the county already owns.

We estimate that opening every branch on Sunday afternoons would cost about **$1.65 million a year**. That is roughly **$2.15 per resident**, about a quarter of one percent of the county's General Fund, and it would add nearly 10% to library opening hours for less than 6% more library spending.

Gwinnett County opens every one of its branches on Sunday from noon to 5. DeKalb can too.

## What is closed, and to whom

A typical DeKalb branch is open 51 hours a week: until 8 on Monday to Wednesday, until 5 on Thursday, Friday and Saturday, and not at all on Sunday. Someone who works a standard day can reach their library on three weekday evenings and on Saturday.

Sunday is the day that schedule fails:

- **Families.** About 164,000 children live in DeKalb. Saturday fills with games, errands and work. Sunday afternoon is when a parent has two free hours and a child has a project due Monday.
- **People who work Saturdays.** Retail, restaurant, health care and delivery workers often have Sunday as their only shared day off.
- **Households that keep a Saturday sabbath.** For observant Jewish and Seventh-day Adventist families, the library is closed for their entire weekend.
- **Students.** Sunday is when homework, test preparation and group projects happen, and it is the day a quiet table and a printer are hardest to find.
- **Homes without internet.** About 6% of DeKalb households have no broadband subscription. Library computers and Wi-Fi are not available to them one day in seven.

## What it costs

We built the estimate from the ground up: staff on duty at each branch, wages from the county's own job postings, a 25% Sunday pay differential, payroll costs, a security guard at every branch, a police officer at Decatur, utilities and cleaning.

| | Base case |
|---|---|
| Every branch, every Sunday, per year | **$1,648,950** |
| One Sunday, all 23 locations | $32,979 |
| Per resident per year | $2.15 |
| Share of the library's $28.5 million budget | 5.8% |
| Share of the $608 million General Fund | 0.27% |
| Open hours gained | 115 a week, a 9.8% increase |

Two checks that do not depend on our staffing assumptions agree. At the library's current staffing cost per open hour, Sunday service comes to about $1.78 million. Using what Montgomery County, Maryland saved when it ended Sunday hours at ten branches, it comes to about $1.81 million. If Sunday somehow cost as much per hour as the entire library does on average, books and administration included, the figure would be $2.7 million. We treat that as the ceiling.

These are our numbers, not the library's. We have asked DeKalb County Public Library for its own, and we will replace ours with theirs.

## Why it is worth it

**The buildings are already paid for.** The county spends $28.5 million a year on libraries, and voters have funded their construction and renovation. Locking them one day in seven leaves that investment idle on the day many residents are most free to use it.

**It is cheap service.** The library averages about 28 visits for every hour a branch is open, at a cost of about $16.50 a visit across its whole budget. If Sunday afternoons drew only average traffic, we estimate each Sunday visit would cost about $10, because the books, the building and the systems are already there. Sunday hours would cost less per visit than the library does today as long as they drew about 60% of average traffic.

**The library's share of the budget has shrunk.** In FY2019 the library received 5.5% of the General Fund. In FY2026 it receives 4.7%, and its budget fell by $910,045 from the year before. Had the library simply kept its 2019 share, it would have about $5 million more this year. Sunday hours would use a third of that.

**Our neighbours do it.** Gwinnett County opens all its branches on Sunday from noon to 5 and keeps them open 59 hours a week, against 51 in DeKalb. Cobb County opens its regional libraries on Sunday afternoons.

**DeKalb has done this before.** In 2016 commissioners added money mid-year to restore night and weekend hours that had been cut in 2011. The 2017 schedule added 111 hours a week.

## Why every branch

A trial at a few branches forces the county to decide which neighbourhoods get Sunday service and which wait. A parent in Lithonia and a parent in Dunwoody have the same Sunday. Six branches would cost about $430,000 and leave 17 closed.

Every branch, opening on the same day, is the fair version and the one all seven commissioners can take home.

## The hard questions

**Where does the money come from, the year after a tax increase?**
From the General Fund, as a permanent line in the library's budget. $1.65 million is 0.27% of that fund. Opening on Sunday, September 12, 2027 would need about $630,000 in the FY2027 budget, with the full amount from FY2028.

**Can the library staff it?**
This is the question that decides whether Sunday service lasts. We propose new part-time Sunday positions, voluntary shifts and a pay differential, so that no current employee is required to give up Sundays. Funding approved in February leaves six months to hire before a September opening. We want the library's leadership to shape this plan, and we will change ours to fit theirs.

**Is it safe?**
The estimate includes a guard at every open branch and the officer already posted at Decatur, about $240,000 a year. Sunday service should meet the same standard as every other day.

**Will anyone come?**
We do not know yet, and we will not pretend to. Experience elsewhere is mixed: some systems find Sunday their busiest day per open hour, others find it quiet. We are counting residents who arrive at locked doors on Sundays and surveying households by ZIP code, and we ask the library to publish Sunday visits every quarter once service begins.

**Would the money do more good elsewhere in the library?**
It is a fair question. Later closing on Thursday and Friday would cost less per hour, because the buildings are already open. Sunday reaches different people: those who cannot come on any weekday or on Saturday. We think the county should be able to afford both over time, and that the day with no service at all comes first.

**Why not trade weekday hours for Sunday hours?**
A swap would close 5,750 weekday hours a year, mostly mornings used by toddlers' story times, seniors and job seekers, and leave the county open no more than it is today.

## What we are asking for

1. **The Board of Commissioners:** fund Sunday service, noon to 5, at every DeKalb County library in the FY2027 budget, as a permanent line.
2. **The Library Board of Trustees:** adopt Sunday hours at every branch as policy and include the cost in the library's budget request.
3. **The Chief Executive Officer:** include Sunday hours in the recommended budget.
4. **The library:** share its own cost estimate and door counts, and help design the staffing plan.

Every library. Every Sunday. Noon to five.
