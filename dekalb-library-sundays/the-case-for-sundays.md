# The case for Sundays

**Open every DeKalb County library on Sunday, noon to 5.**

*Open Sundays DeKalb — working paper, November 2026. Cost figures are the campaign's own estimates, set out in the [cost model](cost-model/). Sources for everything else, and for every figure quoted here, are in [Facts and sources](research/01-facts-and-sources.md).*

## The short version

All 23 DeKalb County libraries are locked every Sunday. For most families that leaves one weekend window, Saturday from 10 to 5, to use buildings and collections the county already owns.

We estimate that opening every branch on Sunday afternoons would cost **between $1.65 million and $2.1 million a year**. That is **between $2 and $3 per resident**, about a third of one percent of the county's General Fund, and it would add nearly 10% to library opening hours.

Gwinnett County opens every one of its 15 branches on Sunday from noon to 5. DeKalb can open its 23.

## What is closed, and to whom

A typical DeKalb branch is open 51 hours a week: until 8 on Monday to Wednesday, until 5 on Thursday, Friday and Saturday, and not at all on Sunday. Someone who works a standard day can reach their library on three weekday evenings and on Saturday.

Sunday is the day that schedule fails:

- **Families.** About 164,000 children live in DeKalb. Saturday fills with games, errands and work. Sunday afternoon is when a parent has two free hours and a child has a project due Monday.
- **People who work Saturdays.** Retail, restaurant, health care and delivery workers often have Sunday as their only shared day off.
- **Households that keep a Saturday sabbath.** For observant Jewish and Seventh-day Adventist families, the library is closed for their entire weekend.
- **Students.** Sunday is when homework, test preparation and group projects happen, and it is the day a quiet table and a printer are hardest to find.
- **Homes without internet.** About 6% of DeKalb households have no broadband subscription. Library computers and Wi-Fi are not available to them one day in seven.

## What it costs

We built the estimate from the ground up: staff on duty at each branch, wages from the county's own job postings, a 25% Sunday pay differential, payroll costs, a security guard at every branch, a police officer at Decatur, utilities and cleaning.

| | Our base estimate | Top of our planning range |
|---|---|---|
| Every branch, every Sunday, per year | **$1.65 million** | **$2.1 million** |
| Per resident per year | $2.15 | $2.78 |
| Share of the $608 million General Fund | 0.27% | 0.35% |
| Share of the library's $28.5 million budget | 5.8% | 7.5% |
| Open hours gained | 115 a week, a 9.8% increase | the same |

**Why a range.** Our base estimate assumes Sunday shifts are filled mostly by new part-time staff. Three other ways of working it out all come in higher:

- If current staff worked Sundays at time and a half, the same plan costs about $1.9 million.
- At the library's present staffing cost for an average open hour, Sunday service comes to about $1.8 million, or $2.1 million with a Sunday differential added.
- What Montgomery County, Maryland saved when it ended Sunday hours at ten branches implies about $1.8 million here. (Montgomery ended those hours for staffing reasons, a warning taken up below.)

A practical ceiling is $2.7 million: what Sunday would cost if every hour were as expensive as the library's average hour, books and administration included. DeKalb's own 2016 restoration of hours, adjusted for inflation, points to the same ceiling.

These are our numbers, not the library's. The biggest assumption, how many staff each branch needs on a Sunday, is our judgment. We will ask DeKalb County Public Library for its figures and replace ours with theirs.

## Why it is worth it

**The buildings are already paid for.** The county spends $28.5 million a year on libraries, and voters approved bonds to build and renovate them. Locking them one day in seven leaves that investment idle on the day many residents are most free to use it.

**Most of the cost of a Sunday visit is already spent.** The library averages about 28 visits for every hour a branch is open. If Sunday afternoons drew that same traffic, each Sunday visit would take about $10 to $13 of new money, against about $16.50 a visit across the library's whole budget today. That comparison flatters Sunday in two ways: it sets new costs against all costs, and some Sunday visitors would otherwise have come on Saturday. We include it to show scale. Sunday would need about 61% of average traffic at our base estimate, and about 79% at the top of the range, to cost no more per visit than the library does now.

**The library's share of the budget has shrunk.** The library's budget has grown 44% since FY2019. The General Fund it is paid from grew 70%. So the library's share fell from 5.5% to 4.7%, and this year its budget is $910,045 smaller than last year's. Had the library kept its 2019 share, it would have about $5 million more. Sunday hours would use less than half of that.

**Some of our neighbours do it.** Gwinnett County opens all 15 of its branches on Sunday from noon to 5 and keeps them open 59 hours a week, against 51 in DeKalb. Cobb County opens its regional libraries on Sunday afternoons. Fulton County's libraries, like DeKalb's, appear to be closed on Sundays.

**DeKalb has done this before.** In 2016 commissioners added money mid-year to restore night and weekend hours that had been cut in 2011. The 2017 schedule added 111 hours a week.

## Why every branch

A trial at a few branches forces the county to decide which neighbourhoods get Sunday service and which wait. A parent in Lithonia and a parent in Dunwoody have the same Sunday. Six branches would cost about $430,000 at our base estimate and leave 17 closed.

A pilot would give the county evidence of demand. Opening every branch and publishing the counts gives better evidence: after a year the county would know how Sunday service performs in every neighbourhood.

Every branch, opening on the same day, is the fair version and the one all seven commissioners can take home.

## The hard questions

**Where does the money come from, the year after a tax increase?**
From the General Fund, as a permanent line in the library's budget. General Fund revenue grew by $48 million in FY2026; Sunday hours would take under 5% of one year's growth. It is a recurring cost and it will rise with wages, as every staffing cost does. We are not proposing to pay for it from reserves or one-time money. Opening on Sunday, September 12, 2027 would need about $630,000 to $780,000 in the FY2027 budget, with the full amount from FY2028.

**Can the library staff it?**
This is the question that decides whether Sunday service lasts, and the answer has to come from the library. We ask the county to fund it so that Sunday work is voluntary and paid at a premium, with new part-time positions carrying most of it. Our estimate includes 10% for supervision and for covering absences. It does not settle who leads each shift, what happens at a small branch when someone is out, or how technical and building support work on a Sunday. Those are the library's to design, with its frontline staff, and our figures will change to fit their plan. Funding approved in February leaves six months to hire before a September opening.

**Is it safe?**
Sunday should run under the same security plan as every other day, including the screening now in place at Decatur. The estimate pays for a guard at every open branch and the officer posted at Decatur, about $240,000 a year. If the library's security plan calls for more, the cost goes up and the ask does not change.

**Will anyone come?**
We do not know yet, and we will not pretend to. Experience elsewhere is mixed: some systems find Sunday their busiest day per open hour, others find it quiet, and Montgomery County, Maryland reduced Sunday service this year. The campaign plans to count residents who arrive at locked doors on Sundays and to survey households by ZIP code, and it asks the library to publish Sunday visits every quarter once service begins.

**Would the money do more good elsewhere in the library?**
It is a fair question. Later closing on Thursday and Friday would cost less per hour, about $180 against $290, because the buildings are already open. Sunday reaches different people: those who cannot come on any weekday or on Saturday. We think the county should be able to afford both over time, and that the day with no service at all comes first.

**Why not trade weekday hours for Sunday hours?**
A swap would close 5,750 weekday hours a year, mostly mornings used by toddlers' story times, seniors and job seekers, and leave the county open no more than it is today.

## What we are asking for

1. **The Board of Commissioners:** fund Sunday service, noon to 5, at every DeKalb County library in the FY2027 budget, as a permanent line.
2. **The Library Board of Trustees:** adopt Sunday hours at every branch as policy and include the cost in the library's budget request.
3. **The Chief Executive Officer:** include Sunday hours in the recommended budget.
4. **The library:** share its own cost estimate and door counts, and design the staffing and security plan with its staff.

Every library. Every Sunday. Noon to five.
