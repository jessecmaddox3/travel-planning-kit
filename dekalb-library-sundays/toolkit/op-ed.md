## DeKalb's libraries are closed the day families need them

[Opening: two or three sentences about a real Sunday. The author standing at a locked library door with a child, or a student with nowhere quiet to work.]

It was not a holiday. It was just Sunday. Every one of DeKalb County's 23 public libraries is closed every Sunday of the year.

Most branches are open 51 hours a week. They close at 5 on Thursdays, Fridays and Saturdays. For anyone with a regular job, that leaves three weekday evenings and one Saturday window. Then Sunday comes, the one afternoon many families have to themselves, and the doors are locked.

Think about who that leaves out. Parents whose Saturdays belong to games and errands. People who work Saturdays in shops, restaurants and hospitals. Families who keep a Saturday sabbath, for whom the library is closed for their entire weekend. Students, whose work is due Monday. And the roughly 6% of DeKalb households with no internet at home, who cannot use a library computer one day in seven.

It does not have to be this way. Cross the line into Gwinnett County and every library is open on Sunday from noon to 5. Cobb County opens its regional libraries on Sunday afternoons.

What would it cost here? A group of residents built an estimate from the county's adopted budget, the library's posted hours and the county's own pay scales. Opening every DeKalb branch from noon to 5 comes to about $1.65 million a year. That includes a pay differential, so Sunday shifts would be voluntary, and a security guard at every branch.

Put another way, it is about $2.15 per resident a year, and about a quarter of one percent of the county's General Fund. It would add nearly 10% to library opening hours for under 6% more library spending. The workings are public, and we have asked the library for its own figures.

DeKalb can afford this. In 2019 the library received 5.5% of the General Fund. This year it receives 4.7%, and its budget was cut by more than $900,000. Had the library only kept the share it had seven years ago, it would have about $5 million more today. Sunday hours would use a third of that.

There are fair questions. Can the library hire the staff? It can if the county funds new part-time Sunday positions and gives it six months to fill them. Would the money be better spent on later weekday hours? Those matter too, but they do nothing for someone who cannot get there on any weekday. Should the county try a few branches first? That means choosing which neighbourhoods get a library on Sunday and which wait. A family in Lithonia has the same Sunday as a family in Dunwoody.

We have done this before. In 2016 commissioners restored night and weekend hours that had been cut in the recession. The libraries opened 111 more hours a week, and the county was better for it.

The county adopts its 2027 budget by the end of February. The chief executive, the seven commissioners and the library's trustees can decide, in the next few weeks, that DeKalb's libraries will open on Sundays.

The buildings are built. The books are on the shelves. We are asking for five hours on a Sunday afternoon.

Every library. Every Sunday. Noon to five.
