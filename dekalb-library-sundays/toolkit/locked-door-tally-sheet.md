# Locked-door tally

**What it is:** a volunteer stands near a library entrance on a Sunday afternoon and counts the people who walk up to the door, find it locked and leave. It turns "nobody would come" into a number.

**When:** Sundays, noon to 5, in one- or two-hour shifts. Count at the same branch on at least two Sundays, and at branches in different parts of the county.

**Rules**
- Stand on the public sidewalk or in the car park, away from the door. Do not block anyone.
- Count each person once. Count people who clearly came for the library: tried the door, read the hours sign, or came to the book drop and looked inside.
- People using only the book return are counted separately.
- Do not photograph anyone without asking. Do not approach children.
- If someone asks what you are doing, tell them and offer the survey QR code.
- A count of zero is a result. Write it down.

### Using the numbers honestly
- A locked-door count is a floor. It misses everyone who already knows the library is closed and stays home.
- Report it as "people counted at the door of [branch] between [times] on [date]", with the hours watched. Do not scale it up to a yearly figure.
- Report low counts alongside high ones.
- A photo of the tally sheet next to the "Closed" sign is worth sharing.
